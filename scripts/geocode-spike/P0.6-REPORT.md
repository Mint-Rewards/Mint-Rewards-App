# P0.6 — Karachi registry expansion

Run 2026-08-25, against the 1,000-point Karachi label sweep from P0.1a.

## What shipped

Karachi's registry goes from **29 areas to 96**, of which **79 are offered** in
the picker. The gap is not waste: it is entries retired from new selection while
staying valid for the users who already hold them, plus neighbourhoods re-levelled
under the town they actually sit in. A `residential` flag, a per-city `COARSE_ADMIN_UNITS` guard, a
`DEPRECATED_TOWNS` mechanism, and six structural invariants enforced by test.

Hidden from new selections: `Askari`, `Garden`, `Shanti Nagar`, `Muslimabad`,
`Mahmudabad`, `Sikandarabad`. Nothing released was deleted — all six remain
valid for the users who already hold them.

`Shanti Nagar` was re-parented under Gulshan-e-Iqbal before being hidden, so
nothing is stranded: residents pick Gulshan-e-Iqbal → Shanti Nagar.
`Sikandarabad` had no such home — Keamari is not a registry town — so it is
retired outright. That trades away an area for its residents in order to make
the `Clifton → Sikandarabad` points resolve to null: silent rather than
confidently wrong, which under prefill is the better failure. It does not fix
the geocoder, which is naming a place ~8 km from the coordinate; those points
remain evidence for keeping prefill off in Clifton.

**Three axes, not one.** A string is independently *selectable* (in the picker),
*resolvable* (the geocoder may prefill it) and *valid as stored* (an existing
profile holding it stays valid). `Bin Qasim Town` is selectable but never
resolvable — a user choosing the parent has ruled the children out, while a
geocoder returning it has ruled nothing out. Deprecated towns are neither
selectable nor resolvable but remain **valid as stored**, and that third column
is what keeps existing profiles out of `LocationUpdateModal`. A future cleanup
tightening `isCanonicalTown` would silently undo it.

**Removal vs deprecation is mechanical, not remembered:** a string may be
removed only if it is absent from `pakistan_locations_baseline.json`; otherwise
it may only be deprecated. `Sharifabad` and `Ancholi` were added this session,
never released, and so were removed outright rather than left as dead entries.

Everything is additive. No existing town, sub-area or city string was edited or
removed, and a new test enforces that permanently (see *Freeze guard* below).

## The provenance sweep

The 531 unmatched labels in `karachi-gaps.md` all came from Google, whose terms
forbid persisting results outside Google services. The registry is permanent, so
those names could not simply be copied in.

`provenance-sweep.js` reverse-geocodes those same 531 coordinates through
LocationIQ (OSM/ODbL, no reuse restriction) and stores the full address object.
531/531 answered, 0 silent, 0 errors — about 11% of one day's free tier.

### Finding 1 — OSM's Karachi coverage is not thin

The plan states that *"19% recall is a data-coverage fact… OSM's suburb-level
data in Karachi is thin"*, and concludes no provider change can help.

On these 531 points, LocationIQ's exact production chain (`suburb ??
neighbourhood`) returns a name at **392/531 (74%)**. Only 26% come back empty.
Across all 1,000 sampled points it names a place at **81%**.

OSM had the data. The registry lacked the entries. That reverses the diagnosis:
the recall ceiling was ours, not the provider's.

### Finding 2 — the OSM list is bigger *and* cleaner than the Google list

113 distinct area names, of which only **4 were already in the registry**. Many
never appear in Google's list at all — Drigh Colony (18 pts), Metroville (17),
Karsaz (16), Gulshan-e-Shamim (16), Ancholi (10). A Google-only triage would
have missed them, and being OSM-sourced they carry no licensing restriction.

Deliberately **excluded**:

- **Korangi Creek (47 pts)** — shares a prefix with Korangi and is a different

  plain "Korangi" at those coordinates. Aliasing it would reproduce the

- **Port of Karachi (13 pts)** — OSM confirmed none of it; it named Sikandarabad
  and Manora instead.

## Results

Registry resolution over the same 1,000 points:

| | before | after |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Google label resolves to a registry entry | 47% | **65%** |
| LocationIQ name resolves to a registry entry | — | **50%** |

The 47% figure was the plan's stated ceiling *for any provider*. It moved 18
points, which is what the expansion was for: a user in Ibrahim Hyderi, Machar
Colony or Bahadurabad can now select their area instead of being pushed to
`townOther` under a hard gate.

## The result that matters more, and it is a caution

Measured against the **same truth basis the original spike used** (Google's
resolved label as truth, uncalibrated):

| | before | after |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| LocationIQ recall | 19% | **19%** |
| LocationIQ precision | 91% | **48%** |
| Wrong area returned | 9 | **97** |

**Recall did not move, and apparent precision halved.** Expansion did not make
the geocoder better at naming a user's area; it made more of its answers
*resolvable*, and those answers are finer-grained than the coarse labels being
scored against.

The disagreements split two ways, and the truth basis cannot tell them apart:

- **Finer but correct** — `Clifton -> Bath Island`, `Orangi Town -> Data Nagar`,
  `Gulshan-e-Iqbal -> Gulshan-e-Shamim`. The new answer is contained in the old.
- **Genuinely wrong** — `Clifton -> Sikandarabad` (Sikandarabad is in Keamari),
  `Korangi -> Mahmudabad`, `Gulshan-e-Iqbal -> Shanti Nagar` (14 pts). Names
  like Shanti Nagar, Mahmudabad and Muslimabad recur in several parts of
  Karachi and collapse onto one registry entry.

### Consequences

1. **`geocodePrefill` stays false everywhere.** A test asserts it. Prefill is
   gated on precision, and precision is now unmeasured rather than merely low.
2. **P3.1 backfill must not bucket users on geocoder agreement.** It would file
   a large share of correctly-addressed users as `disagree`. Use centroid
   distance, per the plan's own open item — this is the evidence that settles it.
3. **Calibration is now on the critical path**, not optional. Real known points
   are the only way to separate finer-but-correct from wrong. It was previously
   judged non-blocking because silence cannot be calibrated away; that reasoning
   no longer holds, because the geocoder is no longer silent.

## Correction: Askari, and the alias rule it exposed

Reviewed by the project owner, who identified that **Askari is not a town** —
Askari 1 through 5 are five separate towns in different parts of Karachi. The
sweep's own coordinates agree: Askari 4 at 24.899,67.117 and Askari 5 at
24.940,67.179, roughly 8 km apart.

Two errors, one structural and one mine:

1. **Mine.** The first pass aliased "Askari 4" and "Askari 5" onto a single
   "Askari" entry, taking `karachi-gaps.md`'s "surface-form alias" bucket at
   face value. This is the same prefix-collision that Korangi Creek was
   *excluded* for — the rule was applied inconsistently. Same error for
   "Garden East"/"Garden West", which are sub-areas of Garden, and for
   "Old Golimar", which OSM names at coordinates distinct from Golimar.

2. **Structural, pre-existing.** The registry modelled Askari as one town with
   Askari 1-5 as sub-areas. Askari 1-5 are now registered as towns.

The bare "Askari" string cannot be deleted — `isLegacyTownValue` would then
treat every profile holding it as stale and force those users through
LocationUpdateModal. So `DEPRECATED_TOWNS` was added, the town-level twin of
the existing sub-area mechanism: hidden from the picker, still valid for
storage, still canonical for validation.

Existing Askari users are **not** stranded. `requiresSubArea` is true for that
town, so they already had to pick Askari 1-5 as a sub-area — their address
carries the discriminator, and P3.1 can map `town: "Askari", subArea: "Askari 3"`
onto `town: "Askari 3"` losslessly.

### The rule, now enforced by test

An alias asserts *these two strings are the same place*. It is valid only when
two sources name the **same coordinate** differently — "Central Jacob Lines" and
OSM's "Jacob Lines" at identical points; "Gulshan e Sikandarabad" and OSM's
"Sikandarabad" at identical points. It is invalid when the name is a finer level
*inside* the target, which is what Askari 4 and Garden East were.

Three tests enforce it across every alias, including the pre-existing ones:
no alias may be a sub-area of its own town; no alias may point at two areas; no
alias may be a canonical town in that city.

### Also found

`getSelectableSubAreasForTown` was wired into nothing — `editProfile.tsx:158`
builds the sub-area picker from `getSubAreasForTown`, so the P0.2 deprecation
set has been inert since it shipped. Left alone deliberately here: sub-area
deprecation is gated on the P0.5 usage count, which is still open. The town
picker at `editProfile.tsx:147` **was** wired to `getSelectableTownsForCity`,
because otherwise this correction would have no effect in the app and users
would keep selecting the value it retires.

## Coarse admin units

`COARSE_ADMIN_UNITS` is keyed **per city**, which is not incidental. "Gulberg
Town" is an administrative parent spanning many areas in Karachi, and in Lahore
"Gulberg" is the area a user picks. A city-blind set silently broke the Lahore
resolution to fix the Karachi one — caught by test. Same for plain "Cantonment",
which is a real, selectable town in Quetta and Hyderabad.

The plan also proposed recovering ~82 points by using the pin plus
`AREA_CENTROIDS` to pick the nearest child area. **Not implemented — measured
and rejected.** Nearest-centroid assignment for these units runs at a 2.4–3.0 km
median distance in a city whose areas are 1–3 km across, because only 16 of
Karachi's areas have a sampled centroid at all. Faisal Cantonment's 58 points
scatter across four areas with no majority. Revisit when centroids exist for most
of Karachi, which needs boundary data or P3.5's collector-verified points.

## Freeze guard

The previous guard snapshotted all of `PAKISTAN_LOCATIONS` and asserted exact
counts. Both trip on any addition, and the fix is to regenerate — which would
regenerate a rename hidden in the same commit right along with it. The guard
approved the one thing it existed to catch.

Replaced with `__tests__/fixtures/pakistan_locations_baseline.json`, captured
before this expansion, plus:

- **every baseline string still exists** (`arrayContaining`) — permits additions,
  forbids edits and deletions, which is the actual invariant;
- **a declared count** that moves only when someone means it to;
- **no duplicate town within a city**, and **no two towns in a city folding to
  the same name** — a fold collision would make `resolveGeocodedName` ambiguous
  forever, and neither string could be edited to break the tie.

## Per-area sample sizes settle the prefill question

The gate is precision >=85% at n>=20, judged **per area**. Measured after the
industrial-suppression rule, only one area clears n>=20:

| area | sampled | prefill-eligible n | correct | wrong | precision |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| **Korangi** | 102 | **55** | 54 | 1 | **98%** |
| Landhi | 29 | 16 | 11 | 5 | 69% |
| PECHS | 27 | 15 | 13 | 2 | 87% |
| Shah Faisal Colony | 52 | 10 | 4 | 6 | 40% |
| Gulshan-e-Iqbal | 38 | 8 | 0 | 8 | 0% |
| Gulistan-e-Jauhar | 33 | 6 | 3 | 3 | 50% |
| DHA | 89 | 3 | 3 | 0 | 100% |
| Clifton | 34 | 1 | 0 | 1 | 0% |

**Korangi clears the gate today** — 98% over n=55, and it holds only one of the
27 open disagreement points, so no adjudication can move it.

**No other area can be promoted at any labelling.** PECHS at 87% is already over
the precision bar and fails only on n=15. Landhi, Shah Faisal, Gulshan-e-Iqbal,
Gulistan-e-Jauhar, DHA and Clifton are all under-sampled, several severely: DHA
was sampled 89 times and yields three eligible answers.

That reorders the remaining work. **The blocker on prefill is sample size, not
labelling** — a denser sweep in the specific areas, not desk adjudication. The
27 open points are still worth an hour for the gazetteer's training labels and
to sanity-check the 77% figure, but they are not on the prefill path.

Note also how thin the eligible column is against `sampled`. Re-parenting is
part of the cause: DHA's Darussalam Society answers now resolve to Korangi
rather than DHA, because Darussalam Society is a Korangi sub-area, not a DHA
one. **Zamzama is different** — corrected below, it is a town-level alias for
DHA, not a sub-area, so it still resolves and now counts as a correct DHA
answer.

### Recompute completeness — a residual gap worth naming

175 of the 1,000 raw points resolve to a registry town under the *current*
registry but have no cached LocationIQ candidate address, so they were

## `subAreaRequired`, and why the default had to invert

`requiresSubArea` used to be derived from *does this town have any sub-areas at
all* — the most aggressive setting available. One child made the step required
for a town's entire population, on a dropdown almost none of them could answer.
That is why `Golimar` and `Tariq Bin Ziyad Colony` had to be retired rather than
re-parented, and why Orangi Town's three entries were trapping one of Asia's
largest informal settlements.

Now a curated flag, defaulting **false**. An override on top of the old
derivation would have failed unsafe — someone adds one sub-area to a childless
town next year and silently makes the step required for everyone in it.
Inverting the default fails safe instead.

Seeded once from the offered lists: `true` where at least 80% of options are
numbered units (`Block 7`, `Phase 3`, `E-7/1`), which is what a systematic
subdivision looks like. **116 of 216 areas require it; the rest do not.** Curated
data from here on, not a live rule — adding one named entry to DHA must not
silently flip it.

The first seeding missed Islamabad, whose sectors subdivide as bare codes
(`E-7/1`) rather than prefixed ones. Only a test caught it.

**Gap audit of the 116.** The seed measures naming *format*, not *coverage* —
three listed blocks out of twenty score 100% numbered. Auditing for gaps in the
numbered series measures coverage instead: 49 contiguous, 9 gapped, 58
inconclusive. Two Karachi entries had real gaps and are now `false`:
`Scheme 33` (10 of Sectors 1-21) and `Naya Nazimabad` (5 of Blocks 1-13). The
rest of the gapped set misses one to three numbers, which a real subdivision
does.

The 58 inconclusive are an artifact of the audit, not thin data: Islamabad's
sub-sectors vary after the slash (`E-10/1..4`) and several registries letter
their blocks rather than numbering them. That is the **third** extractor bug in
three attempts at a mechanical rule over this data — consistent with everything
else here. Stop refining the proxy; the P2.7 fill-rate instrumentation settles
the residue with evidence from actual residents.

Two consequences, both of which unblock deferred work: the 48 OSM singletons
become addable with no further decision, and Orangi's three stop forcing a
question its residents cannot answer.

A weaker case for requiring than it looks, incidentally: `subAreaOther` means
"required" never actually blocks anyone, so requiring the step where coverage is
thin does not produce sub-area data — it produces `subAreaOther` noise.

## One rule disposed of half the open disagreements

**Never prefill a consumer user into a `residential: false` area.** Every
consumer user is a household by construction — B2B sites reach us through
BrandHub and MintTrace, not this app. So a prefill naming SITE, West Wharf or
Korangi Industrial Area is wrong for whoever sees it however precisely the
geocoder placed the coordinate: the pin can sit on a factory while the person
filling the form lives across the road.

Structural, not statistical, so it needs no adjudication and it generalises to
every industrial entry now and in future. It removes **27 of the 54** remaining
sampled disagreements, including the single largest row.

Implemented as `shouldPrefillArea`, which ANDs the precision gate with
`residential` — opening the precision gate on an industrial area still cannot
produce a prefill. Those areas stay selectable and resolvable; only the
pre-selection is suppressed, because a B2B client genuinely at an industrial
site must still be able to say so.

Apparent precision reads **62%** counting every disagreement and **77%** once
the industrial rows are set aside, the latter being the honest figure: those
rows can no longer produce a wrong prefill, so counting them against prefill
precision measures a failure that cannot occur. The gate is 85%.

## The two-level limit, and the one decision it forced

`city -> town -> subArea`. There is no third level and the plan defers adding
one, so **a town with sub-areas cannot be demoted into a sub-area** — its
children would have nowhere to live.

That is the whole reason re-parenting went the way it did. Bahadurabad, Garden
East and Jamshed Quarters had no children, so each moved under Jamshed Town
losslessly. **PECHS is equally a Jamshed Town neighbourhood and was deliberately
left top-level**, because it offers Block 1 through Block 7 and moving it would
replace `PECHS / Block 5` with `Jamshed Town / PECHS` for every future resident
of a dense area, precisely as collections begin needing routing.

Owner decision, 2026-08-25: keep PECHS top-level and accept the structural
inconsistency. A correct hierarchy and a precise address are in tension here;
precision wins. Locked by test, because an inconsistency with no recorded reason
is what a future cleanup quietly "fixes".

The general rule is asserted too: no sub-area may also be a town that still has
children of its own.

## Invariants added, each from a real defect

1. **No alias may be a sub-area of its own town.** `Askari 4` and `Garden East`
   were both aliased onto their parents, asserting a part is the whole.
2. **No name may be both a live town and another live town's sub-area.** Caught
   three duplicates this pass, two of which were removed (`Data Nagar`,
   `Nanakwara`) and one resolved by deprecating the sub-area
   (`Korangi Industrial Area`, which must be a town to carry `residential:false`).
3. **No sub-area may restate its parent town.** `Orangi Town -> Orangi`,
   `Federal B. Area -> B Area`.
4. **Selectable and resolvable are separate axes.** `Bin Qasim Town` is
   deliberately in the picker AND in `COARSE_ADMIN_UNITS`, with the overlap
   asserted so a future audit does not "fix" it.

## The Quetta near-miss

A `COARSE_ADMIN_UNITS` insertion anchored on `"Cantonment"` matched **Quetta's
town list** first — Quetta has a town by that name — briefly making
`Bin Qasim Town` and `Bin Qasim` Quetta towns.

The declared-count assertion caught it (264 vs. 262). This is precisely the case
that retired the snapshot guard: a subset check passes additions by design, so
only a deliberately-declared count catches an *undeclared* one. The old
snapshot-and-count guard would also have failed — but its remedy is
"regenerate", which would have silently blessed two junk towns in Quetta.
